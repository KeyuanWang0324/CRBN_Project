Overview

Can a molecular glue be built to degrade PPIL4?

A structure-based pipeline that designs candidate cereblon (CRBN) glue degraders of PPIL4, a spliceosome component, and ranks them by whether they can plausibly hold a CRBN–ligand–PPIL4 ternary complex together. 2,500 designed analogs enter; 20 come out with full docking, calibration, and provenance.

This is a structural-plausibility enrichment pipeline. It prioritises chemotypes that are geometrically consistent with forming a ternary complex. It is not a validated predictor of degradation or efficacy, and contains no experimental confirmation of PPIL4 recruitment, binding, or anti-cancer activity. Read the rankings as triage, not truth.

Background

The mechanism, and why PPIL4

A molecular glue does not inhibit its target — it destroys it, by forcing a protein the cell had no reason to discard into contact with the machinery that discards things.

01

CRBN is a substrate receptor on an E3 ubiquitin ligase

Cereblon sits in the CRL4 complex, whose job is to tag proteins with ubiquitin and send them to the proteasome. Its pocket normally reads a small set of natural degrons.

02

A glutarimide degron docks a small molecule into that pocket

Thalidomide-class compounds bind CRBN through a glutarimide ring. Every candidate here keeps that ring fixed and varies only what is attached to it — the degron is the part that must not change.

03

The rest of the molecule creates a new surface

Bound to CRBN, the compound presents a composite surface that neither protein has alone. A protein that happens to be complementary to that surface gets recruited — a neosubstrate. This is the glue: the drug does not bind the target on its own.

04

The recruited protein is ubiquitinated and degraded

Held against the ligase, the neosubstrate is tagged and destroyed. Catalytic, and it removes the protein entirely rather than blocking one of its functions.

Why this target

RAS-driven cancers depend on efficient pre-mRNA splicing. Oncogenic RAS raises transcriptional load, and the spliceosome has to keep up — the same “spliceosome addiction” logic established for MYC.

PPIL4 is part of the spliceosome catalytic core. DepMap co-dependency places it with the Prp19/NTC, DHX38 and CDC40 catalytic-activation module, independently of the literature.

PPIL4 is a druggable glue target, and that is already proven. The CRBN–FPFT-2216–PPIL4 ternary complex has been solved experimentally (PDB 9DWV). The question is not whether PPIL4 can be glued to CRBN, but whether a selective glue can be found — FPFT-2216 also degrades IKZF1/3, CK1α, PDE6D and DTWD1, so it cannot test a PPIL4-specific hypothesis.

The honest boundary. PPIL4 is broadly common-essential and is not shown to be KRAS-selective in DepMap knockout data — nor are the validated splicing factors SF3B1 and RBM39, because full-knockout CRISPR cannot resolve this class of selectivity. Any tumour selectivity would have to come from partial-degradation dosing against RAS's transcriptional load, and that remains untested.

Dry lab pipeline

From 2,500 designs to 20 ranked molecules

Each stage narrows the pool and feeds the next. Stage 08 is where the ranking is actually decided; everything before it is a filter, everything after is calibration and assembly.

01

Analog design

2,500 thalidomide analogs generated with the glutarimide degron fixed and the benzo-ring substituents varied.

02–03

CRBN-glue classifiers

Random-forest models on pooled ChEMBL actives, scaffold-split, to drop chemotypes that do not look like CRBN binders.

04

Vina docking

Each candidate docked into CRBN's thalidomide pocket and PPIL4's RRM interface; supplies the starting pose everything downstream uses.

05–06

Ligand-free ternary pre-filter

Coarse HADDOCK3 screens, retained only as a cheap viability check — see the structural-blindness finding in Results.

08

Ligand-inclusive 3-body docking — the ranking stage

CRBN, the ligand and PPIL4 docked as three separate bodies, with the ligand parameterised into the CNS force field so its real chemistry can differentiate candidates. ~70 minutes per molecule.

09

Scoring against the real structure

Every docked molecule re-scored against 9DWV, which is what exposed the DockQ calibration failure.

11–12

Buy list assembly

Ranking, calibration bars, decision rules and registry lookup composed into the final list.

Why the scores are comparable at all

A HADDOCK score only means something within one exact protocol. Two people running “the same” method produce numbers that cannot be ranked against each other. So the protocol is pinned to committed, executable artifacts rather than a prose description: one restraint file identical for every molecule, a fixed random seed, fixed input structures, a pinned HADDOCK3 version, and a fixed output schema.

The restraints deserve specific mention. Earlier versions derived the CRBN-side contact residues from each candidate's own docked pose, which quietly leaked pose noise into the ranking. They are now one committed file for every molecule and control, so the only thing that varies between runs is the molecule itself. There is also no direct CRBN–PPIL4 restraint: the ternary complex is bridged only through the ligand, so the protein–protein interface has to be earned by the molecule rather than imposed.

The controls are the whole point of this chart. A degron-dead negative — a molecule with the CRBN-binding handle deliberately removed, which cannot work — scores −112.2, marginally better than FPFT-2216, the real drug this complex was solved with. So anything scoring above roughly −112 is indistinguishable from a molecule known to be inert, and “beats FPFT-2216” is a meaningless claim.

That is why the bar used here is Z6466608628 at −125.6, the strongest control in the set. Seven of the twenty candidates clear it.

Handed the real drug, does the protocol find the real structure?

The one molecule whose correct answer is independently known is FPFT-2216, the compound the CRBN–PPIL4 ternary complex was solved with. Running it through the identical pipeline reproduces the experimental architecture at DockQ 0.376 — above the CAPRI “acceptable” threshold of 0.23, with an interface RMSD of 2.07 Å and 65% of native contacts recovered.

That validates the method, and only the method. It is one data point, and it says nothing about whether any individual candidate is a real degrader. It is also partly circular: the PPIL4-side restraints are themselves derived from 9DWV's own contact residues, so PPIL4 lands near the true interface partly by construction.

Three results that cut against the pipeline

Each was found by testing a metric the project was already relying on. They constrain what the ranking above can be read to mean.

Discarded metric

A ranking column failed its own calibration

DockQ against the real 9DWV structure looked like a quality score. Measured across all 26 molecules, two negative controls placed 2nd and 3rd — above every candidate. Its correlation with the docking score is +0.055. It is now kept as a geometry diagnostic and is barred from the ranking.

Retired stage

An earlier stage was structurally blind

The first ternary stage never placed the ligand in the force field, so its output was fixed by the contact residues alone. Three separate pairs of different molecules returned byte-identical scores. It was replaced by a stage that docks the ligand as a real third body.

Failed cross-check

Two independent screens disagree

The second screen ranked its own candidates highly. Re-docked here under one protocol, all three sampled molecules landed below every candidate and below the noise floor. The same molecule scores ~14.5 units better under the other protocol — so the two score sets were never comparable.

Rules are independent, so passing count is not a quality score in itself — a molecule can top the docking axis and still fail two rules. The last three rows sit above the noise floor and are carried for completeness only: on score they are not distinguishable from an inert molecule.

Why only four “Buy” calls out of twenty?

Because the rules are independent. The two best-scoring molecules both fail on structure quality: RW_708 has the widest spread across its top ten models of anything docked (sd 12.0) and ranks last of all 21 on the structure axis, so its leading mean is not backed by a convergent funnel; RW_491 also falls below that line. Passing on score alone was judged insufficient. The remaining rows are retained to cover the extremes of each axis, which is what a triage list is for.

Are these compounds purchasable?

Mostly no. Exact-structure lookup against PubChem returns a registry entry for only 3 of the 25 molecules on the list; the rest are novel designs with no CAS number, so acquiring them means custom synthesis. The list also contains five pairs of constitutional isomers, which is why every row is pinned by InChIKey rather than by name or formula.

What this does not show

No experimental validation. PPIL4 has no public CRBN-glue activity data. Every number here is a structural proxy.

The score is size- and interface-biased. Larger molecules with more surface to bury score better regardless of merit, which is exactly why the control set, rather than an absolute number, defines the bar.

The positive control is partly circular. The PPIL4 restraints derive from 9DWV's own contacts. A neutral-restraint control, testing whether the protocol finds the interface unaided, has not been run.

The biological hypothesis is unproven. PPIL4 is broadly common-essential and is not shown to be selectively required in RAS-driven cancers. Any tumour selectivity would have to come from partial-degradation dosing, which remains to be tested.
